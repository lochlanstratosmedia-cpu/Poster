# RBA rate hike guide

## What the RBA decided

On 29 September 2026 the RBA lifted the cash rate by 0.25 percentage points, from 4.35% to 4.60%. It is the fourth increase this year, and the vote was unanimous ([RBA statement](https://www.rba.gov.au/media-releases/2026/mr-26-27.html)).

The Board gave three reasons: inflation is still too high, global energy prices are well above what it assumed in August, and firms are passing on higher costs. It also said it will raise rates again "if needed", so this may not be the last move.

The same statement notes that housing prices have fallen in most capital cities and new housing loans have declined noticeably. That context matters for every group below.

The cash rate is not your loan rate. Each lender decides whether to pass the rise on, by how much, and from what date. Check your lender's announcement or your next repayment notice for the exact figure.

## Homeowners with a mortgage

If your lender passes the rise on in full, a $600,000 variable loan costs about $91 more a month. Across all four rises this year, that same loan is up about $364 a month.

| Loan size (Feb 2026) | Extra per month, this rise | Extra per month, all four 2026 rises |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $500,000 | $76 | $303 |
| $600,000 | $91 | $364 |
| $750,000 | $114 | $454 |
| $1,000,000 | $152 | $606 |

Figures are [Canstar's](https://www.canstar.com.au/news/rba-cash-rate-september-2026-hike-25/) estimates for an owner-occupier paying principal and interest with 25 years left, at the RBA average variable rate. Your number depends on your own rate, balance and term.

What to do this week:

- Find your new repayment. Check your lender's announcement for the new rate and the date it applies. Update your budget before the first higher payment comes out.
- Ask your lender for a better rate. Canstar lists variable rates from 5.69% at smaller lenders. If yours is well above that, a phone call or a refinance quote costs nothing.
- Use your offset or redraw. Money sitting in an offset account cuts the interest you pay at the new higher rate.
- Check your fixed-rate expiry date. Fixed loans aren't affected today. Rolling onto a variable rate later this year or next could be a large jump, so plan for it now.
- Talk to your lender early if it's tight. Lenders have hardship options, and they work best before a payment is missed.

## First home buyers

This rise cuts about $11,200 off what a single buyer on the average full-time wage ($108,650) can borrow. Since January, that buyer has lost about $47,400 of borrowing power, or 9%.

| Buyer | Drop from this rise | Drop since start of 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single, average wage | $11,200 | $47,400 (9%) |
| Couple, two average wages | $22,400 | $94,700 (9%) |

Source: [Canstar](https://www.canstar.com.au/news/what-tuesday-hike-might-mean-for-property/), for a 30-year owner-occupier loan with no debts or dependants. Lenders test you at 3 percentage points above your actual rate, so every rise lowers your ceiling.

Lower prices can offset some of that. Westpac forecasts Sydney prices to fall 10% this year, so in some suburbs prices may drop by more than your budget does. In Brisbane, Adelaide, Perth and Hobart, prices were still up for the year at the end of August.

What to do:

- Get your pre-approval re-checked. An approval issued before today may now be too high. Ask your broker or lender to reconfirm the amount before you bid.
- Leave room for one more rise. ANZ forecasts another increase in November. Work out your repayment at a rate 0.25 to 0.5 points higher and make sure you can still carry it.
- Ask about first home buyer support. Government deposit schemes and state stamp duty concessions can matter more than a 0.25 point rate move. Check what you qualify for in your state.
- Watch your local market, not the headlines. Price moves vary a lot between cities and suburbs. Look at recent sales in the streets you want.

## Buyers and upgraders

Buyers have fewer competitors right now. The RBA says new housing loans have declined noticeably, and REA Group senior economist Eleanor Creagh expects the rise to push prices and sales activity down further ([Daily Mail](https://www.dailymail.com/news/article-16167877/interest-rate-increase-Reserve-Bank-15-year-high.html)).

The catch is that your own budget has shrunk too, by the same amounts shown for first home buyers above.

What to do:

- Reconfirm your finance first. Pre-approvals are based on the rate at the time. Have yours reassessed before you make offers.
- If you're upgrading, sell-first vs buy-first matters more now. In a falling market, buying first risks carrying two loans while your old home sells for less than expected. Talk to your agent about realistic timing and price.
- Use the quieter market to negotiate. Fewer buyers can mean more room on price, longer settlements, or conditions such as building and pest or finance clauses.
- Know the next two dates. September quarter inflation comes out on 28 October and the RBA decides again on 3 November. Either could move rates or buyer confidence.

## Sellers

Every buyer who needs a loan can now borrow less than in January, so sellers are pricing into a smaller pool of finance. How much that bites depends on your city.

| City | Change so far in 2026 (Cotality, to 31 Aug) | Westpac forecast, full 2026 | Westpac forecast, 2027 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sydney | -6.7% | -10% | +2% |
| Melbourne | -6.3% | -8% | +5% |
| Brisbane | +2.8% | +2% | +3% |
| Adelaide | +3.1% | +3% | +4% |
| Perth | +4.1% | +3% | +5% |
| Hobart | +4.2% | +4% | +3% |

Source: Westpac and Cotality, via [Canstar](https://www.canstar.com.au/news/what-tuesday-hike-might-mean-for-property/). Forecasts are opinions and can be wrong.

What to do:

- Price on recent local sales. Buyers have smaller budgets than they did six months ago. A price set on spring 2025 comparables may sit on the market.
- Think about timing. The next RBA decision is 3 November. If you're ready to list, launching before then avoids the risk of another rise landing mid-campaign.
- Make the home easy to finance. Clean building reports and flexible settlement terms help buyers whose finance is under pressure.
- Sort your own next move first. If you're buying again, confirm your borrowing power before you sign an agency agreement.

## Investors

For investors, the rise squeezes cash flow. Rents don't reset when rates do, so the gap between rent and repayments widens until your next lease review.

What to do:

- Rerun the numbers on each property. Put the new rate into your cash flow for every loan, including interest-only loans, where the full rise lands on the repayment straight away.
- Check fixed rates before you lock in. Lenders are already lifting fixed rates. Canstar reports [Macquarie raised its fixed rates twice in three weeks](https://www.canstar.com.au/news/macquarie-hikes-fixed-rates-twice-in-three-weeks/). Fixing gives you certainty, but it's not cheaper by default.
- Review rent at the next lease date. Set rent from comparable listings nearby. Tenants are under cost-of-living pressure too, and a vacancy costs more than a modest increase earns.
- Talk to your accountant. Higher interest changes your tax position on investment loans. Get advice on your own situation before 30 June.
- Look at where prices are still rising. Brisbane, Adelaide, Perth and Hobart were up for 2026 to the end of August, while Sydney and Melbourne were down (see the table above). Buyers with cash or strong finance may find better deals in the falling markets.

## What happens next

The next RBA decision is on 3 November 2026. Before it, the September quarter inflation figures come out on 28 October, and Canstar calls that the number to watch.

The big four banks don't agree on the next move. ANZ forecasts one more rise in November, to 4.85%. CBA, Westpac and NAB expected September to be the last rise this year ([Canstar](https://www.canstar.com.au/news/what-tuesday-hike-might-mean-for-property/)).
